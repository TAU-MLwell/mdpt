Introduction
Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoantibody‐mediated disease in which pathogenic autoantibodies disrupt neuromuscular transmission (Fichtner et al.
2020
). Ocular myasthenia gravis (OMG), classified as Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America (MGFA) Class I and characterized by fluctuating ptosis and diplopia, accounts for approximately 15% of MG cases and significantly impairs quality of life (Gilhus
2016
). Current management begins with acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, which show limited efficacy for diplopia. First‐line immunotherapy consists of oral glucocorticoids, which can rapidly restore ocular motility (Kupersmith et al.
1996
). However, most OMG patients require prolonged prednisone for months or even years to maintain therapeutic efficacy (Verma et al.
2021
), leading to metabolic, skeletal, and infectious complications (Evoli and Damato
2023
; Menon et al.
2021
). For patients with steroid intolerance, dependence, or inadequate response, non‐steroidal immunosuppressive therapies (NSISTs), including azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine, are commonly used as adjunctive or alternative agents (Omata et al.
2019
). However, approximately 20% of patients develop refractory OMG with persistent symptoms.
Biologically targeted therapies, such as neonatal Fc receptor antagonists and complement C5 inhibitors, have demonstrated efficacy in randomized controlled trials for patients with generalized MG (GMG) (Howard et al.
2021
; Marini et al.
2025
, Jin et al.
2025
). However, evidence specifically for OMG remains scarce. Telitacicept is a novel recombinant fusion protein that neutralizes B‐cell activating factor (BAFF) and a proliferation‐inducing ligand (APRIL), thereby inhibiting B lymphocyte maturation and plasma cell survival at multiple stages (Yao et al.
2021
; Dhillon
2021
; Vincent et al.
2013
). Telitacicept was approved for systemic lupus erythematosus in 2021 (Dhillon
2021
) and generalized MG in 2025 (Yin et al.
2024
). A recent case series has suggested potential efficacy of telitacicept in refractory OMG (Lin et al.
2025
), but systematic evaluation in OMG is lacking.
Given the established efficacy of telitacicept in GMG, this study aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy and concurrent corticosteroid reduction in OMG patients. We also compared therapeutic responses between newly/reinitiated treatment and refractory subgroups.
2
Materials and Methods
2.1
Study Design and Patients
This retrospective multicenter study (October 2022–June 2025) enrolled OMG patients from the Departments of Neurology at Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University. Telitacicept was administered to all enrolled patients as either an add‐on or an initial therapy, with regular follow‐up for at least 6 months. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with MG according to the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Myasthenia Gravis (2025 edition) (Chinese Society of Neuroimmunology
2025
) by two experienced neurologists; (2) patients classified as MGFA Class I, with ptosis and/or diplopia and no documented history of generalized MG, MG‐Activities of Daily Living (ADL) score ≥2 at the first visit; (3) with no biologics treatment within previous 3 months. (4) Previous immunotherapy regimens remained unchanged before telitacicept treatment: corticosteroids at a stable dose for at least 1 month and NSISTs (tacrolimus or azathioprine) at the same dose for at least 3 months. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.
Patients were classified into refractory OMG (rOMG) and newly/reinitiated treatment OMG (nOMG) groups according to prior treatment history. We defined rOMG as persistent ocular symptoms despite stable background immunotherapy. The nOMG group was operationally defined as comprising patients who were either newly diagnosed with ocular‐onset MG and had never received MG‐directed treatment or had not received
no
MG‐directed treatment for at least 2 years before enrollment. Telitacicept was added to ongoing background immunotherapy in the rOMG group, whereas the nOMG group received oral prednisone and telitacicept concurrently.
2.2
Assessments
Initially, patients received telitacicept at 160  or 240 mg once weekly (QW), or 160 mg every 2 weeks (Q2W). Based on periodic clinical evaluations and individualized therapeutic responses, we progressively extended or adjusted dosing intervals and regimens during follow‐up, while gradually reducing concurrent prednisone doses. Detailed patient‐specific telitacicept dosing trajectories and interval adjustments across the 6 months are provided in Table
S1
.
We retrospectively collected basic clinical data, including sex, age at MG onset, clinical presentation, disease duration, MG antibody status, thymic status, history of thymectomy, baseline prednisone dose, and baseline NSIST use. For patients with a history of thymectomy, the timing of surgery relative to telitacicept initiation was extracted from medical records. Notably, all thymectomies were performed at least 5 years before telitacicept initiation (Tables
2
and
3
).
All patients were followed up for at least 6 months. Clinical outcomes were assessed at weeks 1, 2, 3, and 4, and at months 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 post‐treatment initiation. At each follow‐up visit, we recorded MG‐ADL score, daily prednisone dosage, and adverse events. Minimal symptom expression (MSE) was defined as an MG‐ADL score of 0 or 1 (Fang et al.
2025
). Cumulative response rate (CRR) was defined as the proportion of patients who had achieved a ≥1‐point reduction in the MG‐ADL score from baseline at or before each assessment. If a patient maintains MSE for a certain period, we define this period as the duration of sustained MSE. The duration of sustained MSE was calculated as the total continuous period during which patients remained in MSE status without relapse.
2.3
Endpoints
The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving MSE within 6 months. Secondary efficacy endpoints assessed at each time point from baseline to month 6 included (1) change in MG‐ADL score, (2) reduction in mean daily prednisone dosage, (3) CRR at each observation time point, (4) proportion of patients achieving a daily prednisone dose≤5 mg at month 6, and (5) duration of sustained MSE.
2.4
Adverse Events and Short‐Term Tolerability
We recorded and described all adverse events occurring during the treatment period 2 using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0.
2.5
Statistical Analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 and GraphPad Prism 10.1.2. Descriptive statistics summarized baseline characteristics; continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (IQR) based on distribution (Shapiro–Wilk test), and categorical variables were presented as
n
(%) with chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests. Group comparisons of continuous variables used the Mann–Whitney U test. The proportion of patients achieving MSE was analyzed using Kaplan–Meier curves. Longitudinal changes were assessed using repeated‐measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) (normal distribution) or the Friedman test (non‐parametric). Linear mixed models compared MG‐ADL and prednisone trajectories between groups, with time as a continuous fixed effect, group as a categorical fixed effect, their interaction, and patient‐specific random intercepts. All tests were two‐sided, with significance set at
p
< 0.05.

3.2
Overall Treatment Response in Patients Receiving Telitacicept‐Based Regimens
In the 19 patients with OMG treated with telitacicept, marked clinical improvement and a significant corticosteroid‐tapering outcomes effect were observed after 6 months of treatment. As shown in the Kaplan–Meier curve, the proportion of patients achieving MSE increased progressively over time: seven patients (36.84%) reached MSE within 2 months, nine patients (47.36%) reached MSE within 3 months, 11 patients (57.89%) reached MSE within 4 months, and 13 patients (68.42%) reached MSE at the end of observation. The remaining six patients (three rOMG and three nOMG) showed obvious clinical improvement but still experienced mild symptoms at the last follow‐up (Figure
1A
). No participant had uncontrolled MG symptoms. Among the 13 patients who achieved MSE, the median duration of sustained MSE was 3.00 (IQR 2.00–4.00) months, ranging from 0.25 to 6 months.

Throughout the 6‐month follow‐up period, no serious adverse events or events leading to treatment discontinuation were observed. The only recorded adverse events were mild injection‐site reactions (erythema and transient localized pain), which occurred in three patients (15.79%) during the first month of therapy. All events were Grade 1 per CTCAE v5.0 and resolved spontaneously without dose interruption or specific medical management. Detailed safety event profiles are summarized in Table

3.5
Exploratory Subgroup Analyses by Prior Treatment History
To further explore the potential impact of prior treatment history on clinical responses to telitacicept, an exploratory comparison of treatment trajectories was conducted between the rOMG and nOMG groups.
3.5.1
MG‐ADL Score Trajectories

Discussion
In this multicenter real‐world cohort, telitacicept was associated with rapid and sustained clinical improvement in OMG, along with a progressive reduction in daily prednisone doses. By 6 months, 68.4% of the patients achieved MSE, and 84.2% were receiving ≤5 mg/day—a target widely recognized as the optimal long‐term maintenance dose in MG management (Imai et al.
2020
; Narayanaswami et al.
2021
). All patients demonstrated an initial clinical response within 5 weeks of treatment initiation. This is the first study to evaluate telitacicept in OMG stratified by prior treatment history. Notably, nOMG exhibited a trend toward faster early improvement; however, no statistically significant difference in outcomes was detected between the two groups. LMM analysis of the entire 6‐month trajectory detected no significant difference in the overall rate of improvement between the groups. These findings suggest that telitacicept‐based regimens may represent a promising treatment strategy for OMG, enabling concurrent corticosteroid reduction across the spectrum of prior treatment experience.
An early clinical response was observed following telitacicept treatment, with all 19 patients achieving an initial response within 5 weeks and 26.3% showing improvement as early as week 1. This trajectory aligns with the phase 2 GMG trial by Yin et al. (
2024
), which reported significant clinical improvements by week 4.
While direct cross‐study comparisons are limited by differences in patient populations and study designs, our observed 68.4% MSE rate aligns well with, or numerically surpasses, rates reported for conventional immunosuppressants (45.2%), eculizumab (21.4%), and efgartigimod (44.6%) in GMG (Bril et al.
2024
; Vissing et al.
2020
; Uzawa et al.
2023
), as well as the 33.3% reported in a Bayesian study of telitacicept in GMG (Duan and Xiao
2026
). This is the first study to evaluate MSE as a prespecified endpoint in OMG and to examine MSE rates stratified by prior treatment history. We also introduced “duration of sustained MSE” as a novel endpoint; among 13 MSE achievers, nine maintained MSE for >3 months, and two for up to 6 months. The observed sustained improvement may be biologically consistent with dual BAFF/APRIL blockade; however, the absence of immunological biomarker data precludes conclusions regarding the underlying mechanism (Yao et al.
2021
; Vincent et al.
2013
).
It is worth noting that all patients but one in our study adopted a low‐dose or moderate‐dose prednisone regimen combined with telitacicept. So, the rapid onset of our regimen should be interpreted as the effect of the combined immunomodulatory strategy, rather than a monotherapy effect of telitacicept. However, as widely documented in the literature, conventional low‐dose corticosteroids in OMG typically require 14 weeks to achieve clinical response (Benatar et al.
2016
), which is significantly slower than the onset time observed in our study. It suggests that telitacicept may have played a significant role in the combined regimen.
Achieving minimal corticosteroid exposure is a key therapeutic goal. Prolonged steroid use drives metabolic and skeletal morbidity (Evoli and Damato
2023
; Menon et al.
2021
; Kaminski and Denk
2022
). Our 75% prednisone reduction at 6 months, with 84.2% reaching ≤5 mg/day, aligns with the 76% reduction reported in a Bayesian GMG study (Duan and Xiao
2026
), and the slow tapering achieved with traditional agents, which require 6–18 months (Farmakidis et al.
2018
). Initial prednisone doses varied from 10 to 60 mg/day and were lower in the nOMG group than in the rOMG group (median 17.5 mg/day vs. 30.0 mg/day). Clinical improvement was observed in both groups; however, because prednisone dosing and tapering were not standardized, these findings cannot establish whether lower starting doses are sufficient when combined with telitacicept. This possibility warrants prospective evaluation.
Subgroup analysis showed final MSE proportions of 72.7% in the rOMG group and 62.5% in theChinese Society of Neuroimmunology. 2025. “Chinese Guideline for Diagnosis and Treatment of Myasthenia Gravis (2025 Edition).” Chinese Journal of Neurology 58, no. 7: 721–741. [In Chinese] nOMG group; no statistically significant difference in outcomes was detected between the two groups. LMM analysis of the entire 6‐month trajectory detected no significant difference in the overall rate of improvement. However, given our small sample size and baseline imbalances, this exploratory subgroup comparison is indeed statistically underpowered. Previous studies support biological heterogeneity among antibody‐producing cell populations and the role of BAFF/APRIL‐family signaling in B‐cell and plasma‐cell survival (Farmakidis et al.
2018
; Croft
2009
; Lazaridis and Tzartos
2020
). Despite delayed ADL response, the refractory group showed aggressive early steroid tapering (83.3% by week 12), underscoring telitacicept's capacity (Wu et al.
2025
).
Conceptually, telitacicept differs from downstream inhibitors (complement inhibitors, neonatal Fc receptor [FcRn] antagonists) that provide rapid but often non‐durable relief. Real‐world data show 35.9% of patients discontinued efgartigimod due to insufficient efficacy (Fuchs et al.
2026
), and cyclical IgG fluctuations require individualized dosing (Habib et al.
2025
; Konno et al.
2024
). Evidence for FcRn antagonists in OMG remains sparse (Ma et al.
2024
). Rituximab is limited by CD20‐negative long‐lived plasma cells that evade depletion (Menon and Bhandari
2025
). Hypothetically, dual BAFF/APRIL inhibition by telitacicept may address this limitation by depriving these cells of key survival signals (Hiepe and Radbruch
2016
), which could theoretically contribute to more sustained control. However, whether this proposed cellular mechanism accounts for the clinical observations in our cohort requires further validation with immunological biomarkers. Short‐term tolerability in this cohort was acceptable, consistent with pivotal trials (Yao et al.
2021
; Yin et al.
2024
), with only mild injection‐site reactions.
Limitations include small sample size, short follow‐up, and medication heterogeneity and baseline imbalances (particularly disease duration), potential underreporting of adverse events due to retrospective design, and LMM's assumption of linear trajectories. The lack of a control group precludes direct comparison with standard therapies. The concurrent initiation of prednisone and telitacicept in the newly/reinitiated treatment OMG group introduces substantial confounding. The subgroup comparisons are strictly exploratory generating. Despite this, targeted biologic development in OMG is advancing, with ongoing Phase 3 trials of efgartigimod (
NCT06558279
) and telitacicept (
NCT07249632
). Our study provides the first real‐world data on telitacicept in OMG stratified by prior treatment history, complementing these registration trials. Further research should involve designing a randomized controlled study, selecting patients (untreated or refractory), standardizing the treatment plan (such as whether to combine prednisone or not), and extending the observation period to assess risk of generalization.
In conclusion, in this small multicenter retrospective cohort, telitacicept‐based regimens were associated with improvement in MG‐ADL scores and reduced prednisone exposure over 6 months, with no serious adverse events documented. No statistically significant differences in clinical or prednisone‐dose trajectories were detected between the groups defined by prior treatment history. Prospective controlled studies are required to confirm these findings.
